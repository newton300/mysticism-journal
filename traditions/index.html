Voices across the centuries

A living inheritance.

Meet the writers and ideas that continue to open doors into the inner life.

Rumi — the language of longing

Jalal al-Din Rumi's poetry moves through love, separation, bewilderment, and the possibility of transformation. His images are vivid and generous: a reed flute, a guest house, a turning sky. Beneath them lies a persistent invitation to look beyond appearances and listen for what the heart cannot easily explain.

Attar — the courage to travel

Farid ud-Din Attar shaped spiritual questions into unforgettable journeys. In his tales, the search for wisdom often begins with confusion and proceeds through encounters that expose vanity, fear, and attachment. The road itself becomes a mirror.

Saadi — wisdom in the world

Saadi Shirazi brings moral insight into the texture of everyday life. His stories observe generosity and self-deception, dignity and pretence, with a clarity that can be both tender and unsparing. The spiritual question is never far from the way we treat one another.

Reading with context and care

Sufi literature spans languages, regions, genres, and centuries. No single interpretation can contain it. Our aim is to explore particular texts attentively, distinguish literary reading from historical fact, and make space for the richness and complexity of this inheritance.
